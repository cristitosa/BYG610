0.1 What the course is about

Regional planning is about making collective decisions over large, complex territories — decisions that affect where people live, how they move, what gets built, and what futures are possible. This course focuses on one of the most interesting challenges in that work: how do planners think about and plan for futures they cannot predict?

We use Rogaland and its live FRAM planning process as our laboratory. FRAM — the County Plan for Regional Development, Land Use and Mobility — is being developed by Rogaland Fylkeskommune right now, pointing towards 2050. This course runs in parallel with that real process, giving you direct access to the people, documents, and debates that shape regional planning practice.

0.2 Course structure at a glance

The course moves through three phases:

Phase Sessions Focus
Foundations 1 – 5 What is a region? The Norwegian planning system. Getting to know Rogaland and FRAM.
Futures methods 6 – 10 Four approaches to scenario-building: forecasting, exploratory, normative, backcasting.
Project work 11 – 13 Applying methods. Working on your final assignment.

0.3 The final assignment

The course culminates in a written assignment (4,500 words individual / 7,000 words group) in which you apply one or more futures methods to a planning question you define, using Rogaland as your case context. See the Assignment section for full guidance, methodology support, and annotated examples.
