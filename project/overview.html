12  Assignment Overview

What the final project asks of you

12.1 What you are being asked to do

The final assignment is a structured analytical piece in which you:

  1. Identify a planning question relevant to Rogaland’s regional future
  2. Situate it within relevant theory and literature
  3. Apply one or more futures methods from the course
  4. Analyse what the method reveals about your question
  5. Draw conclusions with implications for planning practice

This is not a descriptive report about Rogaland. It is an argument — you are making a case, using methods, supported by evidence.

NoteFormal requirements
Individual Group
Word limit 4,500 words 7,000 words
Line spacing 1.5 1.5
Font size 11pt 11pt
Excludes References, tables, figures References, tables, figures

12.2 Required structure

Your assignment must follow this structure:

  1. Front page — title, course name/number, full name(s), student number(s)
  2. Table of contents
  3. Introduction and problem area — what is the issue, why does it matter?
  4. Research question(s) — precise, answerable, scoped to your method
  5. Literature review and theory — what do scholars say? What conceptual framework guides you?
  6. Methodology — which futures method(s) are you using and why? How did you apply them?
  7. Case description — what is the specific Rogaland context you are analysing?
  8. Analysis — this is the core: applying your method to your question
  9. Conclusion — what did you find? What does it mean for planning?
  10. References
  11. Appendices (if needed)

Detailed guidance for each section is in Assignment Instructions.

12.3 Choosing your research question

Your question should be:

  • Spatial — it concerns a territory, a place, a planning challenge in Rogaland
  • Futures-oriented — it cannot be answered by description alone; it requires reasoning about uncertainty or possibility
  • Methodologically tractable — you can actually apply one of the course methods to it
  • Scoped — not so broad that 4,500 words cannot do it justice

See Research Questions for examples and a step-by-step guide to sharpening your question.

12.4 Which method to choose

You are not required to use all four methods. One well-applied method is better than four superficially mentioned ones. The choice depends on your question:

If your question is about… Consider…
What is likely to happen (baseline) Trend/forecast scenario
What could happen under uncertainty Exploratory scenarios
What should happen (values, vision) Normative scenarios
How to get from a desired future to the present Backcasting
Comparing alternative trajectories Trend + exploratory in sequence

See Methodology Guide for detailed guidance on applying each method in a written assignment.

12.5 Examples

Two annotated example assignments are available in Examples. Read them carefully before Session 11.
